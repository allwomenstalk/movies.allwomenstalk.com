# From Squid Game to The Remarried Empress: 15 Asian Dramas for Your Watchlist

Sometimes you want a thriller that makes every small decision feel dangerous. Sometimes you want an empress to walk away from a terrible marriage with her dignity—and a much better plan. And sometimes the most appealing story is about an ordinary person finally finding the confidence to speak up at work.

This Asian drama watchlist makes room for all three. Alongside **Squid Game** and **Alice in Borderland**, there are Thai culinary investigations, a Taiwanese emergency department, Japanese school games, and Chinese adventures built around folklore and political danger.

The aim is to help you choose by mood, premise, and commitment. This spoiler-light guide brings together released seasons, current shows, and clearly marked previews. Release information was checked on **November 19, 2026**, and streaming availability varies by country.

## Big titles, very different kinds of tension

### 1. The Remarried Empress: a divorce becomes a power move

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-01-1795083799877_718x1024.jpg)

_Promotional artwork; source supplied in the research brief. [Image source](https://i0.wp.com/nosleep4dramas.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/remarried.jpg?resize=718%2C1024&ssl=1)._

A royal breakup is rarely private. In **The Remarried Empress**, Emperor Sovieshu’s affair threatens Navier’s marriage and her position, giving a familiar relationship crisis consequences that extend across an empire. Her response supplies the hook: she will accept the divorce if she is allowed to remarry rival prince Heinrey.

That makes this an appealing premise for viewers drawn to heroines who have to think politically as well as emotionally. A new relationship could offer escape, leverage, or the chance to choose a different life; the tension lies in how those possibilities fit together.

Shin Mina plays Navier, alongside Ju Jihoon, Lee Jongsuk, and Lee Seyoung. You do not need to have read the NAVER WEBTOON to understand the central dilemma: what happens when the person expected to absorb a public humiliation refuses to cooperate?

Disney confirms a November 3 launch, with the finale scheduled for November 25. As of this guide’s date, it is still a developing story rather than a completed-season binge.

### 2. Squid Game, season 2: Gi-hun returns with a different purpose

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-10-1795083812898_1013x1500.jpg)

_Promotional image: Netflix. [Image source](https://dnm.nflximg.net/api/v6/a01MIaQTUgSJoLaS_OCn68Eg3hU/AAAAQdXYUYCohYH4LUycVz6a3YJH45JhFUWW15ZWrG_xJd5EVzSoXT52qzq9p-XICB3YvT8ibF8QYAJqzY78Kbt7ueV2DSO4uSCU8TQc2Q9GliCx9PfrR9hOZcQwtoX-bu4ILcsiF8DZsLVEClXK_Sx7G7_J.jpg?r=101)._

Returning to the games changes meaning when the protagonist knows what they are. Season two sends Gi-hun back into the competition determined to destroy the system, creating a different starting point from the desperate hope that brought him there originally.

For a viewer choosing what to watch next, that shift matters more than the promise of unfamiliar challenges. The premise puts individual conviction up against incentives that encourage people to keep participating. Voting, alliances, and group pressure become part of the danger alongside the games themselves.

Lee Jung-jae returns as Gi-hun, with Lee Byung-hun and Wi Ha-jun among the central cast. The seven-episode season belongs in sequence after season one; its stakes depend on what Gi-hun has already experienced.

Choose this when you want to continue the series’ argument about debt, power, and the choices people make under pressure. Plan to follow it with the final season, and avoid cast explainers or recap videos if you want late-season developments preserved.

If you are catching up, watch seasons two and three close enough together to retain the emotional and practical consequences of the earlier episodes. You will have more room to form your own response if you save ending explainers and finale debates until afterward.

### 4. Alice in Borderland, season 3: back to the world of dangerous games

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-07-1795083808551_1200x675.jpg)

_Promotional image: Netflix. [Image source](https://dnm.nflximg.net/api/v6/a01MIaQTUgSJoLaS_OCn68Eg3hU/AAAAQa-gwSXH2BNH1-U39k1bo_yzF-IODayxnrNMsNeEbVCihLY7sMF4vrtAzYRGtrwmo7kTJsyQBkDhajEJU_ssymlwtoakt5EisgNH-LJTbHtBiWq3L_vcfhhyCOXLYkAomaALmcjVc5MseWFzao0syhcT.jpg?r=db3)._

If the appeal of a survival thriller is working through its rules, **Alice in Borderland** deserves a place beside Squid Game on your list. Season three brings Arisu back to the Borderland after Usagi disappears into it, with the Joker at the center of the next stage.

Kento Yamazaki and Tao Tsuchiya return, and Shinsuke Sato continues as director. The series’ roots are in Haro Aso’s manga, but newcomers should begin with the television story’s earlier seasons rather than treating this as a standalone puzzle box.

The premise raises an immediate emotional question: after finding a way forward, what could pull someone back into such a dangerous place? That connection between attachment and survival gives you something to follow beyond the mechanics of each challenge.

Released in September 2025, this is a catch-up option rather than a new November 2026 premiere. Put it on when you want an established fictional world and have the attention to follow both its games and its returning relationships.

## Discoveries beyond the biggest franchises

### 5. My Chef in Crime: when the kitchen becomes part of the investigation

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-02-1795083801956_681x1024.jpg)

_Promotional artwork; source supplied in the research brief. [Image source](https://i0.wp.com/nosleep4dramas.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/my-chef-in-crime.jpg?resize=681%2C1024&ssl=1)._

Food chemistry and forensic science make an intriguing pairing. **My Chef in Crime** follows a former forensic scientist who has become a chef, only to find himself suspected in a rival chef’s death. Clearing his name brings him into partnership with a police investigator.

Jeff Satur and Nuttanicha Dungwattanawanich lead the Thai series. Its distinctive invitation is to follow someone whose professional knowledge could transform an everyday ingredient or cooking process into evidence.

If you enjoy trying to reach a deduction before the detective, this is a premise to investigate. The particularly interesting possibility is that cooking may become part of the reasoning, giving the culinary setting a practical role beyond attractive dishes and restaurant atmosphere.

The show also offers a route into Thai drama for viewers who tend to choose by genre rather than country. A crime-solving partnership gives you a familiar starting point while the food-science angle supplies something less familiar.

TVmaze lists the series as airing on TrueVisions NOW. Check English subtitles and access in your country before building an evening around it; an available Thai platform listing does not establish an international release.

### 6. Miracles of the ER: finding your place while everyone needs you

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-03-1795083803622_819x1024.jpg)

_Promotional artwork; source supplied in the research brief. [Image source](https://i0.wp.com/nosleep4dramas.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Miracles-of-the-ER-819x1024.jpg?resize=819%2C1024&ssl=1)._

Starting a demanding job is difficult enough without the sense that everyone else has better credentials. **Miracles of the ER** takes that insecurity into a Taiwanese emergency department, where an average medical graduate is chosen by a brilliant doctor and must establish himself among elite peers.

Wallace Huo, Cheryl Yang, and Kent Tsai feature in the ensemble. Netflix lists eight episodes, making this a manageable option if you want a new workplace setting without committing to a sprawling series.

The rookie perspective is the useful point of entry. You can approach the hospital through someone still learning how to function there, with professional confidence and belonging as immediate personal stakes.

Netflix’s episode descriptions also identify malpractice disputes and financial obstacles to care. Those subjects suggest a workplace story concerned with the pressures surrounding treatment as well as emergency cases. They are a guide to its themes, rather than evidence of medical accuracy.

Try this premise when you want urgency grounded in people doing a difficult job together. The appeal is in the combination of practical responsibility, hierarchy, and the need to keep working while confidence is still catching up.

### 7. Rock, Paper, Landmine: clever games, school-life consequences

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-04-1795083805648_1920x1080.jpg)

_©2026 Amazon Content Services LLC or its Affiliates. Source: THE SEVEN. [Image source](https://images.microcms-assets.io/assets/14a7d8016d314002bb5f08852b1dd335/9531e4aef4d74b37b8dbe75f8d117bb3/JIGL_Main%20Visual_PR.jpg?w=1920)._

The title sounds threatening, but there is an essential distinction to make before pressing play: **Rock, Paper, Landmine** is built around nonlethal competition. Its characters wager things that matter intensely within their school lives, including money, friendships, and opportunities.

Nana Mori plays Mato Imoriya, a seemingly relaxed student with an exceptional instinct for games. She enters underground competitions to help her friend Kazahi, played by Aju Makita. Familiar games acquire added rules, creating openings for strategy and misdirection.

For fans who love the reasoning in survival dramas, that is an interesting change of scale. A contest does not need a death toll to make understanding an opponent feel urgent. The premise invites you to pay attention to both the formal rules and the relationships surrounding the table.

Directed by Kazuaki Seki and adapted from Yugo Aosaki’s novel, the series launched worldwide on Prime Video on November 13, according to producer THE SEVEN.

Choose it when your main craving is a battle of wits. The friendship at its center offers a personal reason to care about the outcome beyond the pleasure of a clever solution.

### 8. The Ordinary Jackpot: enough money to behave differently

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-08-1795083809663_480x270.jpg)

_Promotional image supplied by Viki. [Image source](https://1.vikiplatform.com/c/41650c/a019b21494.jpg?e=t&q=g&s=480x270&x=b)._

What if winning the lottery changed your working life without ending it? In **The Ordinary Jackpot**, office worker Gong Eun-tae wins 1.3 billion won and keeps his job. The money gives him a cushion, changing how he approaches decisions and relationships.

Lee Jun-hyuk plays Eun-tae, with Seo Hyun-woo and Oh Dae-hwan among the cast. Adapted from Seo In Ha’s web novel, the setup turns financial security into a source of workplace disruption.

Its appeal is easy to imagine after a frustrating day: how would you act if the possibility of losing your job felt less frightening? The premise makes room for changes in confidence that can be smaller—and more recognizable—than abandoning your old life entirely.

This is the list’s clearest alternative for readers drawn to office dynamics and everyday power imbalances. Check [Viki’s official listing](https://www.viki.com/tv/41650c-the-ordinary-jackpot) for regional availability and subtitles; [Apple TV’s catalog](https://tv.apple.com/us/show/the-ordinary-jackpot/umc.cmc.br0dvrrvc0e3r64utsf6mq51) provides additional synopsis and credits.

### 9. The Guilty: a shared past that refuses to stay buried

![](https://img.allw.mn/uploads/asian-drama-watchlist-09-1795083811127_640x896.jpg)

_Promotional artwork; source supplied in the research brief. [Image source](https://www.cpophome.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/The-Guilty.jpg)._

**The Guilty** takes the list into Chinese crime drama, connecting an investigation in the present with earlier wrongdoing and the intertwined lives of several young people. Wei Daxun and Sun Qian star alongside Wang Longzheng and Gao Zhiting.

The hook is the relationship between loyalty and responsibility. When people share a painful history, protecting one another may also mean keeping truths out of reach. That is the kind of moral complication to look for if your favorite mysteries are built around relationships as much as evidence.

The series is listed as a 14-episode iQIYI drama, directed by Fu Dongyu and Wang Taotao. Its contained length may suit viewers looking for a different crime-story setting without beginning a lengthy historical series.

Content note: sexual assault and violence; check episode-level advisories if those affect your viewing choices. For the premise and credits, see [CPOP HOME’s series page](https://www.cpophome.com/the-guilty-wei-daxun-sun-qian/) or [MyDramaList](https://mydramalist.com/762569-wu-zui-zhi-shen).

### 10. Crash Season 2: Fury Road: a bigger remit for the traffic-crime team

_Promotional artwork; source supplied in the research brief. [Image source](https://i0.wp.com/nosleep4dramas.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/crash-22.jpg?resize=719%2C1024&ssl=1)._

Roads offer a specific setting for crime stories: the evidence moves, the danger can spread quickly, and an apparently ordinary incident may involve more than it first suggests. **Crash Season 2: Fury Road** returns to traffic-crime investigations with the team operating on a national scale.

The cast listing includes Lee Min-ki, Kwak Sun-young, and Heo Sung-tae. For returning viewers, the draw is seeing that group apply its expertise to a broader range of cases. For newcomers, season one is the sensible introduction to the team and its working relationships.

This belongs on your radar if an investigative ensemble and vehicle-based action sound more appealing than a solitary detective. The expanded remit supplies a reason for the story to move beyond familiar territory while retaining a particular professional focus.

### 11. The Blue Road: two detectives, one disturbing trail

**Preview: Netflix has announced a November 20 premiere.**

A murder investigation that crosses borders creates difficulties beyond finding the next clue. **The Blue Road** pairs Seoul detective Kim Seok, played by Son Suk-ku, with Tokyo detective Inose, played by Eita Nagayama, as connected killings bring their paths together.

Netflix’s announcement describes distorted bodies and cryptic messages at the center of the case. Director Han Jun-hee leads a cast that also includes Kim Shin-rock, Jung Jae-young, and Choi Sung-eun.

This is the darker crime option to bookmark if the combination of an uneasy partnership and an international investigation appeals to you. Differences in method, jurisdiction, and personal purpose give the setup several possible sources of friction before the mystery itself is resolved.

As of November 19, this entry is a preview. The promotional premise establishes the intended territory, but a trailer cannot establish how well the finished series develops its partnership or sustains its mystery. If disturbing crime imagery is something you avoid, read the platform’s advisories first.

### 12. Xin Liao Zhai / Liao Zhai: supernatural stories with human concerns

Drawn from Pu Songling’s **Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio**, this anthology centers encounters between people and supernatural beings. Desire, temptation, and moral uncertainty make it a different prospect from a single continuing romance or investigation.

An anthology also changes how you might watch: individual stories can become their own points of interest. Cast members are organized by arc, so a performer associated with one tale should not automatically be treated as the lead of the whole production.

Keep this on your list if folklore appeals to you, especially stories that complicate the boundary between human and monstrous behavior. A confirmed premiere date was not established in the material checked.

### 15. River of No Return: fifteen days to reach the capital

A deadline makes the premise of **River of No Return** immediately tangible. Crown prince Zhu Zhanji is caught in a conspiracy in Nanjing and must reach the capital along the Grand Canal within fifteen days, traveling with a constable, a doctor, and a junior official.

Cheng Yi and Lin Gengxin feature in the cast. The iQIYI listing identifies an adaptation of Ma Boyong’s novel, directed by Zhang Li and An Wei.

Bookmark this if your favorite historical adventures involve a group on the move, shifting alliances, and obstacles that consume precious time. The platform listing does not establish a firm premiere day in the checked material, so it remains a preview here.

## What should you put on tonight?

Start with the kind of attention you want to give a story. For continuing franchise stakes, follow **Squid Game** or **Alice in Borderland** in season order. For a new premise, consider the royal maneuvering of **The Remarried Empress**, the game logic of **Rock, Paper, Landmine**, or the professional pressure of **Miracles of the ER**.

If you want something closer to everyday frustrations, **The Ordinary Jackpot** offers the most relatable starting question: what would change if you had a little more room to say no?
